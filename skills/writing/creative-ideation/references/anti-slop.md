# Anti-slop rules

Slop is what comes out when a model averages over its training data. The defining test: the idea
could have been written for a different prompt by changing one noun. Apply these to every idea set.

## Signatures to reject

- Trendy pairings: "AI-powered X", "blockchain Y", "Uber for Z". Two fashionable nouns joined.
- The safe four domains (productivity, fitness, food, travel) with no specific friction named.
- Vague abstractions: "a platform that connects people who want X with people who offer X".
- A solution in search of a problem: "what if we used AR to...".
- Familiar startup shapes: two-sided marketplace, subscription box, social network for a niche.
- Buzzwords: empowers, seamless, leverage, innovative, cutting-edge, revolutionary, unlock, holistic,
  ecosystem, journey, game-changing, powerful.
- Generic fiction or essay settings: a small town, an unlikely friendship, "the changing nature of X
  in the digital age".
- Five ideas of identical shape and length.
- Invented-word product names with dropped vowels.
- Marketing tone: "this is exciting because".

## Five-question check

1. Could this idea be for a different prompt after changing a noun? If yes, it is slop.
2. Does it name actual people, places, materials or mechanisms? If not, slop.
3. Is at least one element surprising enough to need explaining? If not, slop.
4. Can you describe how it would feel to use, read or experience, in concrete terms? If not, slop.
5. Would a sharp friend in this field be embarrassed to pitch it? If yes, slop.

Pass all five. Two failures means rewrite.

## Techniques

1. **Refuse the first three** ideas (five on saturated topics). Generate and discard them, then
   generate the ones you will show.
2. **Force specificity.** Replace abstractions with real proper nouns, and "an app" with a medium.
   A tech stack is not specificity; a concrete mechanism is.
3. **Weirdness budget.** At least one element of each idea, such as the medium, the audience or the
   unit of measure, needs explaining. If everything is conventional, reject. If everything is odd,
   you have gone too far.
4. **Avoid trending combinations.** If both halves were in the tech press recently, swap one for
   something obscure, dated, or from a foreign field.
5. **Use real references**, but only ones you know exist. Do not invent a work, person or quote.
6. **State the failure mode.** Real ideas have problems; say what they are.
7. **Refuse the round number.** Use three or seven. Vary the shape of each idea.
8. **Drop the sales voice.** Flat, specific, sometimes self-deprecating.
9. **Name the material.** Code in a language, paper in a format, sound on an instrument, a room of a
   given size.
10. **Fiction and essays** go somewhere people do not usually write about, with a concrete place.

## Self-check before sending

- No buzzwords from the list.
- At least one specific proper noun or mechanism per idea.
- At least one odd element per idea, and at least one idea you could start today.
- No two ideas share a shape; no five-item list of equal length.
- No "exciting" framing. The medium and material are stated.

If three or more fail, regenerate. When the owner asks for "simple", give a constrained idea that
takes an afternoon, not a generic one.
